## OVERVIEW

BioMedica is Zenith's largest healthcare corporation and the Monolith responsible for Lamda. Its operations include hospitals, clinics, medical research, healthcare plans, and the manufacture of medical products.

BioMedica is known for its medical services and the effectiveness of its treatments. Its hospitals are well-funded, and its research has produced significant medical advancements. 

Healthcare plans are available at varying levels of cost and coverage. Monolith employees commonly receive healthcare benefits through their employment, making corporate positions particularly attractive. However, coverage can be expensive for lower-income individuals, and uninsured patients face substantially higher costs.

## HISTORY

BioMedica grew through its success in healthcare and medical research. Its hospitals and medical products established its reputation, while its services became increasingly important to the other Monoliths.

As more Monoliths began offering BioMedica healthcare plans as an employee benefit, the corporation gained a broad customer base and lasting business relationships. Healthcare coverage became a common employment benefit across corporate Zenith. Many children grow up hearing "Get a job at a Monolith, you'll need the care when you're older!"

Its growing resources supported further research and development, allowing BioMedica to expand its medical capabilities and operations throughout the Zones. After the previous Monolith in Lamda, Greenthumb, faced a massive financial crash-- Biomedica swooped in and took their place, the former floundering into a lower level company, and shell of its former self. 

Lamda now serves as the corporation's primary base and a major center for medical professionals and retirees.

## WORKING FOR BIOMEDICA

Working for BioMedica is considered a respectable career, particularly for doctors, researchers, and other qualified professionals. 

Not everyone works in medicine. BioMedica also needs administrators, technicians, maintenance staff, factory workers, and other support personnel to keep its operations running. 

The company has a reputation for high standards and demanding work. Most employees take pride in contributing to medical progress, though the full scope of BioMedica's research is not known to everyone working there.

Many staff- From higher to lower, are known to be suddenly 'moved' into other locations, for classified or patented projects. Do too good of a job, and you might just catch the executives eye. 

## CULTURE

BioMedica maintains a professional and quiet workplace culture. Employees are expected to meet high standards and maintain the corporation's high reputation for competence.

Employment is considered prestigious, particularly for doctors, researchers, and other highly qualified professionals. These positions can offer strong compensation, healthcare benefits, and opportunities for advancement.

Many employees take pride in their work and regard medical research as an important contribution to human health. Because of this and the above, The corporation's has very demanding standards and a competitive enviroment.

## RUMORS

BioMedica has a good reputation, and most people have little reason to question it. Still, its size and influence have inspired the occasional rumor.

- **Missing Patients**: Every so often, someone claims a relative was transferred to a BioMedica facility and never heard from again. Usually, the patient was already seriously ill, and the paperwork appears to be in order.
- **The Long-Term Residents**: Some residents of Lamda's more expensive retirement communities seem to age remarkably well. People joke about unusually good healthcare, though a few insist there's more to it.
- **Private Clinics**: BioMedica operates facilities that don't appear in its public directories. Most people assume they're reserved for sensitive research or wealthy clients. Nobody seems to know what actually happens inside.
- **Experimental Treatment**: Stories circulate about an experimental procedure that can reverse conditions thought to be permanent. Supposedly, it works. Getting access to it is another matter entirely.
- **The Research Wing**: Former low-level employees occasionally allude to research divisions with rules far stricter than the rest of the company. Some claim even senior staff aren't told what their colleagues are working on.
- **The Fine Print**: BioMedica's healthcare contracts are extrodinarily long and complex, some say containing containing clauses that most customers never notice or understand until it's too late. Nobody seems to agree on what those clauses actually permit
